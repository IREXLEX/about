IMPORTANT STATUS AND USE NOTICE

No single “Articles of Organization” filing creates or authorizes a law firm worldwide. Legal entities are formed under the law of a particular state or country, and the right to render legal services is separately controlled by each jurisdiction, bar regulator, court, tribunal, and arbitral institution. Part I is a jurisdiction-neutral drafting form for adaptation to the chosen home jurisdiction. It must not be filed until locally licensed counsel has selected the correct entity type, conformed the language to the official form and governing statute, confirmed ownership and naming rules, and completed every bracketed field.

California note: If the firm’s home office will be in California, do not use this PLLC form without California counsel’s written confirmation. California permits law practices through forms that include registered limited liability partnerships and law corporations, subject to Secretary of State and State Bar requirements. The appropriate California instrument would be a separate LLP registration or professional-corporation package, not these generic PLLC articles.

Drafting disclaimer. This document is a drafting starting point, not legal advice, a legal opinion, a filing guarantee, or evidence of authority to practice in any jurisdiction. It does not create an attorney-client relationship. Laws, professional rules, tribunal procedures, tax treatment, beneficial-ownership duties, insurance requirements, and filing forms change. Licensed counsel in every relevant jurisdiction must approve the structure and text before formation, filing, marketing, client intake, or service delivery.

2.Part II — International Practice Framework

A global practice is a network of permissions, not one filing

IREXLEX should treat cross-border authority as a layered system. The home entity supplies legal personality. It does not itself authorize local-law practice, establish an office abroad, confer rights of audience, or satisfy a tribunal’s accreditation rules. Each matter must pass through the following gates before services begin.

  1. Home-entity gate. Confirm that the chosen entity form may provide legal services, that its name is approved, and that ownership and management are lawful.

  2. Foreign-presence gate. Determine whether the contemplated activity constitutes doing business, opening an office, maintaining a permanent establishment, or triggering tax, employment, immigration, registration, or insurance duties.

  3. Professional-authorization gate. Confirm that each lawyer may perform the proposed work, whether through full admission, temporary practice, pro hac vice admission, registered foreign legal consultant status, or association with local counsel.

  4. Forum gate. Confirm the rules of the specific court, tribunal, arbitral institution, administrative body, or appointing authority.

  5. Matter-risk gate. Complete conflicts, client identity, beneficial ownership, source-of-funds, sanctions, anti-corruption, data-transfer, privilege, security, and reputational-risk review.

  6. Engagement gate. Execute a written engagement defining client identity, scope, jurisdictions, responsible lawyers, local-counsel roles, fees, trust arrangements, governing law, dispute terms, data handling, and termination rights.

Jurisdiction-entry memorandum

Before IREXLEX markets or delivers services in a new jurisdiction, a locally reviewed memorandum should answer:

  1. Which entity types may law firms use, and must the entity or branch be registered with a professional regulator?

  2. Who may own, vote, manage, share fees with, or use the firm’s name?

  3. Does the activity require foreign qualification, branch registration, a local office, a local partner, or a foreign legal consultant registration?

  4. Which activities are reserved to locally admitted lawyers, and what temporary-practice or association exceptions exist?

  5. What professional-indemnity insurance, client-protection fund, bond, guarantee, or minimum-capital requirements apply?

  6. What rules govern advertising, titles, fee agreements, contingency fees, referral fees, multidisciplinary practice, and fee sharing?

  7. What rules govern client money, interest, banking location, currency, reconciliation, and audit?

  8. What taxes, payroll duties, permanent-establishment risks, immigration permissions, and employment rules apply?

  9. What confidentiality, professional secrecy, privilege, discovery, interception, localization, cybersecurity, and cross-border data-transfer rules apply?

  10. What client due diligence, beneficial-ownership, suspicious-activity reporting, sanctions, export-control, anti-bribery, and anti-corruption duties apply?

  11. Which regulator receives complaints, and what local record-retention, inspection, and reporting duties apply?

  12. What closure, successor-counsel, file-transfer, and client-notification duties apply?

Permitted delivery models

  1. Foreign qualification of the home entity. Use only when the destination jurisdiction permits the selected professional entity and its ownership structure.

  2. Locally formed affiliate. Use when a separate locally regulated entity is required. Adopt intercompany terms that preserve professional independence and confidentiality.

  3. Registered foreign legal consultant. Limit advice to the law of the lawyer’s admitted jurisdiction and other expressly permitted fields; do not imply local-law authority.

  4. Independent local-counsel association. Define who represents the client, who controls strategy, who appears, how fees are allocated, who holds funds, and how privilege and conflicts are handled.

  5. Temporary or matter-specific admission. Obtain pro hac vice, temporary-practice, or tribunal permission before reserved work begins.

  6. International arbitration team. Confirm the seat’s mandatory law, applicable institutional rules, counsel restrictions, ethical duties, and enforcement strategy even when local bar admission is not required.

Forum-specific accreditation and representation

International Court of Justice

The International Court of Justice (ICJ) hears contentious cases between States and gives advisory opinions when properly requested. A law firm cannot be a party. A State appearing before the Court appoints an Agent and may be assisted by counsel and advocates. IREXLEX may support a State only under a valid sovereign engagement and appointment structure, with conflict, confidentiality, authority, diplomatic, and security protocols confirmed for the particular proceeding. Jurisdiction depends on State consent; firm marketing must not imply a general right to commence proceedings for private clients. [1]

International Criminal Court

Counsel before the International Criminal Court (ICC) must satisfy the Court’s applicable qualifications and procedural requirements. Rule 22 of the ICC Rules of Procedure and Evidence requires established competence in international or criminal law and procedure, relevant experience, and fluency in at least one working language; retained defense counsel must file a power of attorney with the Registrar. IREXLEX shall verify current list, appointment, code-of-conduct, legal-aid, support-team, and filing requirements before accepting an ICC role. [2]

International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea

Practice before the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea (ITLOS) is governed by the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea, the Tribunal’s Statute and Rules, and case-specific directions. States and other eligible entities act through designated Agents and may be assisted by counsel and advocates. IREXLEX shall confirm party eligibility, written authority, filing and translation requirements, confidentiality, and the composition of the delegation for each case. [3]

ICSID proceedings

Under the International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID) Arbitration Rules, a party may be represented or assisted by agents, counsel, advocates, or other advisers, and names plus proof of authority must be promptly notified to the Secretary-General. IREXLEX shall separately confirm jurisdiction under the applicable treaty, contract, investment law, and ICSID Convention or Additional Facility framework; authority to represent a party does not establish tribunal jurisdiction. [4]

UNCITRAL and other international arbitration

The United Nations Commission on International Trade Law (UNCITRAL) Arbitration Rules address representation and assistance and permit a tribunal to require proof of authority. For every arbitration, IREXLEX shall verify the agreed rules, seat, governing law, appointing authority, counsel-conduct rules, disclosure duties, confidentiality regime, evidence protocol, language, hearing logistics, and enforcement jurisdictions. The seat’s mandatory law and any institutional rules may impose additional requirements. [5]

Domestic and regional courts

Admission before a domestic, regional, supranational, or administrative court is forum-specific. Before appearance or filing, IREXLEX shall obtain admission, rights of audience, sponsorship, local-counsel participation, electronic-filing credentials, translations, powers of attorney, notarization, legalization, apostille, and service formalities as applicable.

Cross-border ethical conflict protocol

When potentially applicable rules differ, the responsible partner shall identify every connected jurisdiction and forum; determine the governing conduct rule; compare confidentiality, privilege, conflicts, fee, advertising, supervision, withdrawal, and reporting duties; seek local ethics advice if necessary; record the analysis; and adopt a lawful solution that protects the client without misrepresenting authority.

No lawyer shall use the stricter-rule principle mechanically if it would violate another jurisdiction’s mandatory law. The escalation decision belongs to the General Counsel or Ethics Partner, with independent advice where the firm’s own interests are implicated.

Privilege and confidentiality protocol

At matter opening, the team shall map the client, relevant affiliates, legal roles, third-party advisers, data locations, expected forums, and discovery regimes. Communications shall be labeled accurately, not reflexively. Business advice and policy consulting shall be segregated from legal advice when privilege treatment may differ. Local counsel shall confirm whether in-house counsel, foreign lawyers, patent professionals, consultants, translators, experts, and public-relations advisers fall within local privilege or professional-secrecy rules.

Data protection and technology controls

For each matter, IREXLEX shall document the lawful basis for processing, controller and processor roles, data minimization, retention, access restrictions, breach response, and cross-border transfer mechanism. European Union personal data transferred to a non-European Economic Area destination may require an adequacy basis, approved safeguards such as Standard Contractual Clauses, a transfer impact assessment, and supplementary measures. The applicable regime must be assessed rather than assumed. [6]

The firm shall maintain an approved-vendor register. Engagement of cloud, e-discovery, translation, investigation, communications, or artificial-intelligence services requires security, confidentiality, data-location, subcontracting, deletion, incident-notice, audit, and lawful-process review. Client information shall not be entered into a public or consumer AI system unless the client has authorized the use and the firm has approved the system and safeguards.

Anti-money-laundering, sanctions, and integrity controls

The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) standards contemplate customer due diligence and recordkeeping for lawyers and other legal professionals when they undertake specified transactional activities, but implementation differs by country. IREXLEX shall map the actual law of each jurisdiction, including whether and when legal professional privilege or professional secrecy affects reporting. [7]

Before engagement and before material payments or transfers, the firm shall use a risk-based procedure to identify the client and authorized representatives; establish beneficial ownership where applicable; understand the purpose of the engagement; screen relevant persons, entities, jurisdictions, vessels, assets, and banks against applicable sanctions; evaluate source of funds and source of wealth where warranted; assess corruption and public-official exposure; and document approval or rejection.

A sanctions issue shall be evaluated under every regime with a sufficient connection to the matter, including the nationality and location of personnel, currency and clearing routes, service providers, and data or technology exports. Screening is not a substitute for legal analysis of ownership, control, sectoral measures, general licenses, exceptions, or reporting duties.

Engagement and local-counsel protocol

Every cross-border engagement letter should identify:

  1. The exact client and any nonclient constituents.

  2. The scope, exclusions, objectives, and jurisdictions covered.

  3. The lawyers responsible for each body of law and forum.

  4. Whether local counsel is co-counsel, separately retained, or a subcontractor, subject to local rules.

  5. Fee basis, estimates, taxes, currency, exchange risk, referral arrangements, and responsibility for third-party costs.

  6. Trust-account and payment instructions, including the entity receiving funds.

  7. Governing law, forum, arbitration or mediation terms, and mandatory client rights.

  8. Confidentiality, privilege limits, data locations, cross-border transfers, technology use, and security expectations.

  9. Conflicts, informed consents, organizational-client issues, and future-conflict provisions where lawful.

  10. Document preservation, retention, file ownership, return, and destruction.

  11. Client cooperation, authority, communications, complaints, termination, and withdrawal.

International operations control cycle

**Before launch:** Obtain the jurisdiction memorandum, choose the lawful delivery model, register the entity and professionals, place insurance, approve banking and data systems, train personnel, and approve public descriptions of the practice.

**Before each matter:** Complete authority, conflicts, identity, sanctions, scope, staffing, competence, local-counsel, data, fee, trust, and forum checks.

**During each matter:** Monitor legal changes, conflicts, sanctions, deadlines, funds, data access, delegation, and client instructions; refresh diligence when facts change.

**At closure:** Deliver the closing letter, account for funds, transfer or return property, preserve required records, release holds when authorized, restrict access, and calendar post-matter duties.

**At least annually:** Review every jurisdictional authorization, professional status, insurance policy, branch registration, trust account, vendor, training program, marketing statement, and policy exception.



3.Part III — Services & Policy Architecture

Service design standard

Every IREXLEX service line shall have a written charter stating its purpose, permitted jurisdictions, responsible partner, required competencies, intake questions, mandatory approvals, local-counsel triggers, standard deliverables, exclusions, data classification, sanctions and anti-money-laundering risk, fee controls, and quality-review process. No service description may promise outcomes, universal availability, or authority the firm does not possess.

Public international law and State representation

Scope. Treaty interpretation, State responsibility, sovereign immunity, diplomatic and consular law, boundary and maritime issues, peaceful settlement, advisory support, and public-international-law opinions.

Formalities. Confirm the identity and authority of the State organ instructing the firm, public-procurement and sovereign-approval requirements, capacity to waive immunity or privilege, document classification, diplomatic channels, and the jurisdictional basis of any contemplated proceeding.

Control. Representation before the ICJ or ITLOS requires case-specific sovereign appointment and compliance with the forum’s rules; private clients cannot be presented as parties where the constitutive instrument limits standing.

International arbitration and investment disputes

Scope. Treaty and contract analysis, dispute prevention, notices, negotiation, mediation, ICSID and UNCITRAL proceedings, institutional arbitration, enforcement, and award-risk strategy.

Formalities. Verify consent, standing, nationality, investment definition, treaty or contract preconditions, cooling-off periods, limitation issues, seat, rules, governing law, language, authority documents, funding disclosure, arbitrator and counsel conflicts, interim relief, and enforcement jurisdictions.

Control. An investment-dispute merits opinion shall be separate from a representation-authority check. Funding, insurance, sanctions, and asset-tracing issues require specialized review.

International criminal law and investigations

Scope. Defense, victims’ representation where permitted, internal investigations, evidence assessment, cooperation requests, international-crimes analysis, and accountability-mechanism support.

Formalities. Confirm forum jurisdiction, counsel eligibility, appointment or power of attorney, legal-aid rules, investigator licensing, witness protection, evidence custody, trauma-informed protocols, translation, classified material, and disclosure obligations.

Control. Prosecutorial or special-counsel work may be undertaken only under lawful appointment by a competent authority and with structural conflict separation from defense work.

Constitutional, administrative, and public law

Scope. Constitutional analysis, judicial review, civil liberties, public authority powers, emergency measures, administrative procedure, and institutional reform.

Formalities. Use locally admitted counsel for local-law opinions and appearances; verify standing, exhaustion, notice, limitation, sovereign-immunity, record, and remedy rules.

Corporate, commercial, and cross-border transactions

Scope. Entity formation, governance, contracts, joint ventures, acquisitions, restructurings, beneficial ownership, and cross-border market entry.

Formalities. Map local corporate capacity, foreign investment screening, competition, tax, labor, licensing, anti-corruption, exchange control, notarization, legalization, filing, translation, and beneficial-ownership requirements.

Control. IREXLEX shall not act as nominee, escrow holder, corporate service provider, or funds conduit without separate approval and all required licensing and controls.

Banking, finance, fintech, and investment regulation

Scope. Licensing strategy, prudential and conduct regulation, payments, digital assets, investment products, governance, enforcement response, and regulatory policy.

Formalities. Identify each regulated activity, customer location, booking entity, asset or instrument, payment rail, custody arrangement, marketing channel, and responsible regulator. Obtain specialist advice on licensing, consumer protection, capital, safeguarding, cybersecurity, financial promotions, market conduct, and resolution.

Control. Legal advice is not regulatory approval. No client-facing product may be described as licensed, insured, guaranteed, or compliant without documentary confirmation from the responsible regulatory team.

Sanctions, export controls, anti-corruption, and financial crime

Scope. Risk assessments, screening design, licensing analysis, investigations, disclosures, remediation, transaction diligence, and compliance programs.

Formalities. Determine all jurisdictions with a nexus; assess persons, ownership and control, sectors, goods, technology, services, destinations, currencies, vessels, and intermediaries; document licenses and exceptions; and preserve confidentiality while observing mandatory reporting law.

Control. High-risk work requires written approval from the Sanctions and Anti-Money-Laundering Lead and the responsible partner. No advice may be structured to conceal ownership, evade controls, obstruct reporting, or facilitate prohibited conduct.

International human rights law

Scope. Advice and representation concerning the Universal Declaration of Human Rights; the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights; the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights; other applicable universal treaties; and domestic implementation. Services may include strategic litigation, legal opinions, urgent-protection strategy, fact development, reparations analysis, institutional reform, and submissions to authorized national, regional, and international mechanisms. [14] [19]

United Nations mechanisms. Subject to the relevant State’s treaty status, acceptance of complaint procedures, standing, admissibility, exhaustion, time limits, and the mechanism’s current rules, the firm may support State reporting, stakeholder submissions, individual communications, inquiries, and engagement with treaty bodies. It may also support carefully documented communications and submissions to applicable Human Rights Council special procedures. No engagement shall imply that a mechanism has jurisdiction, will accept a submission, or can grant a particular remedy. [15]

Regional systems. Matters involving the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights or Inter-American Court of Human Rights, the European Court of Human Rights, or the African Commission on Human and Peoples’ Rights and related African mechanisms require a system-specific authorization memorandum. The memorandum shall address the controlling instrument, respondent-state obligations, standing, victim status, exhaustion, filing deadlines, interim or precautionary measures, representation rules, language, remedies, enforcement, and any route from commission to court. [16] [17] [18]

Business and human rights. The firm may advise States, enterprises, investors, civil-society organizations, affected persons, and institutions on human-rights due diligence, impact assessment, supply-chain and project risk, stakeholder engagement, grievance mechanisms, remedy, disclosure, investigations, and governance under applicable law and the United Nations Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights. Engagements shall distinguish binding legal duties from nonbinding standards and shall not treat a policy, audit, or due-diligence exercise as a guarantee that harm will not occur. [19]

Formalities and controls. Before accepting a matter, the firm shall confirm mandate, client identity, authority, forum, applicable instruments, jurisdiction, admissibility, exhaustion, deadlines, informed consent, conflicts, funding, evidence integrity, witness and survivor protection, digital and physical security, confidentiality, privilege, reprisals risk, and do-no-harm measures. Public advocacy, publication, media engagement, and disclosure of a client’s identity require separate written authorization and a documented safety review.

International humanitarian law and humanitarian representation

Scope. Armed-conflict classification, conduct-of-hostilities analysis, detention, occupation, protection of civilians and other protected persons, humanitarian access, missing-person and family-link issues, sanctions and export-control exceptions for humanitarian activity, accountability, reparations, and institutional policy.

Integration with human rights law. A conflict-related matter shall identify separately the applicable international humanitarian law, international human rights law, refugee law, international criminal law, domestic law, and forum rules. The team shall analyze their concurrent or complementary application without assuming that one body of law displaces another.

Humanitarian representation. Representation of affected persons, humanitarian organizations, medical or relief actors, and other eligible clients may include access negotiations, detention and protection advice, documentation, submissions, and remedy strategy. Every engagement shall preserve humanitarian principles, operational security, informed consent, confidentiality, evidence integrity, and the safety and independence of clients, witnesses, staff, and local partners.

Control. Public-interest or humanitarian purpose does not relax competence, authority, conflicts, sanctions, funding, security, or evidence obligations. The responsible partner shall coordinate any overlap with the international criminal, sanctions, human rights, and policy-architecture teams.

Criminal, civil, appellate, and extraordinary-remedy practice

Scope. Criminal defense; civil liability; tort, personal injury, and complex litigation; appeals; writs; injunctions; and extraordinary remedies.

Formalities. Each matter requires local admission or properly approved participation, a conflicts check, deadline and limitation review, authority to act, fee-compliance review, preservation notice, evidence plan, and forum-specific filing protocol.

Control. “Nationwide” or “global” descriptions may be used only with a conspicuous explanation that services are delivered by lawyers admitted in the relevant jurisdiction or in association with authorized local counsel.

Wills, trusts, estates, and cross-border private client

Scope. Estate planning, succession, trusts, probate coordination, capacity planning, and cross-border asset structuring.

Formalities. Determine domicile, residence, nationality, situs of assets, family status, forced-heirship rules, tax exposure, trust recognition, execution formalities, translation, notarization, apostille, and professional conflicts among family members.

Control. Tax and local-law advice shall be assigned to qualified advisers, and the engagement shall identify who is and is not the client.

Policy architecture and institutional design

Scope. Governance charters, legislation and regulations, institutional mandates, regulatory frameworks, compliance systems, standard operating procedures, public consultation, implementation roadmaps, impact assessment, and monitoring frameworks.

Method. Each policy engagement shall proceed through mandate definition; stakeholder and rights mapping; legal-authority analysis; comparative research; options and trade-off analysis; draft architecture; consultation; implementation planning; accountability design; metrics; review; and amendment procedures.

Required design elements. Every policy package should identify legal authority, purpose, scope, definitions, decision rights, separation of duties, eligibility and exclusion criteria, due process, records, transparency, confidentiality, appeals, complaints, oversight, enforcement, funding, implementation dates, training, data governance, evaluation metrics, and sunset or review provisions.

Human-rights integration. Where a policy may affect rights-holders, the design process shall identify applicable rights and duty-bearers; assess disparate, cumulative, and conflict-related impacts; provide safe and meaningful participation; define prevention and mitigation measures; preserve access to remedy; and establish indicators for implementation and accountability. Any reliance on the United Nations Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights or another nonbinding framework shall be distinguished from mandatory legal duties.

Policy architecture: mandatory firm controls

Conflicts of interest

The conflicts system shall search clients, affiliates, beneficial owners, adverse parties, witnesses, experts, counsel, funders, insurers, key personnel, and material interests using all known names and scripts. It shall record the search, analysis, approvals, consent basis, information barriers, and continuing-monitoring triggers. No matter opens until clearance is documented.

Confidentiality and privilege

Access shall be need-based. Teams shall use approved communication, storage, translation, investigation, and collaboration systems. External disclosure requires authority and a privilege analysis. Ethical walls shall be used only where legally recognized and operationally effective.

Engagement letters and scope control

No substantive work begins without a signed engagement or documented emergency authorization. Scope changes, new parties, new jurisdictions, appeals, enforcement, investigations, and public communications require written amendments and renewed conflicts and risk review.

Trust accounting and client property

The responsible lawyer shall identify the governing trust-account rule, approved bank, currency, signatories, reconciliation schedule, payment authority, interest treatment, and audit trail. The firm shall prohibit commingling, informal pass-through arrangements, and disbursement before cleared funds and authorization.

Document retention, legal holds, and file disposition

Retention periods shall be assigned by matter type and jurisdiction. A legal hold overrides routine destruction. Closure procedures shall address original instruments, client access, data export, vendor deletion, backup limitations, and confidential destruction.

Supervision, competence, and quality assurance

Every matter shall have a responsible partner and a staffing plan. The firm shall verify competence, workload, language ability, local authorization, and supervision. Material advice, filings, settlement authority, public statements, and transfer of client funds shall receive the approvals specified in the matter plan.

Complaints, errors, and claims

The firm shall provide a clear complaint channel, preserve evidence, notify management and insurers when required, protect limitation periods, avoid prejudicing the client, and obtain independent advice when the firm’s interests diverge from the client’s.

Pro bono and public-interest commitments

The firm shall adopt a written pro bono policy approved by management. The policy shall define eligibility, priority areas, intake authority, annual planning, supervision, competence, conflicts, expenses, local-counsel needs, continuity, closure, and impact review. Pro bono and reduced-fee matters shall receive the same professional, confidentiality, security, quality, and risk controls as fee-paying matters. No aspirational target, geographic reach, beneficiary category, or resource commitment shall be announced until formally approved and supportable.

Human-rights and humanitarian pro bono work shall be selected through transparent criteria that consider legal merit, urgency, vulnerability, access to remedy, potential reprisals, operational safety, available competence, and sustainable staffing. Case selection shall not discriminate unlawfully, create a duty to accept matters, imply guaranteed representation, or compromise professional independence. Strategic litigation, amicus work, treaty-body submissions, institutional assistance, training, and policy projects may qualify when approved under the policy.

Artificial intelligence and legal technology

Technology may assist but shall not replace professional judgment, source verification, confidentiality safeguards, conflicts controls, or lawyer supervision. The firm shall inventory approved systems, prohibit unapproved client-data use, test material outputs, document human review, manage vendor and model risk, and disclose material use when law, tribunal rule, contract, or client instruction requires.

Business continuity and incident response

The firm shall maintain tested plans for cyber incidents, data loss, unavailable personnel, office disruption, sanctions changes, bank interruption, political instability, and urgent court deadlines. Plans shall identify decision authority, client notice standards, regulator and insurer notice, secure alternatives, and recovery priorities.

Policy exceptions and review

Exceptions require written reasons, risk ownership, compensating controls, expiry, and approval by the responsible policy owner plus the General Counsel or Ethics Partner. Policies shall be reviewed at least annually and sooner after legal change, incident, audit finding, office launch, or new service line.
